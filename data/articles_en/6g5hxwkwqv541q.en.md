# [Shocking] Meta Claims "Uploading Pirated Content is Fair Use"!? The Legal Battle Shaping the Future of AI Training is Heating Up

AI engineers, did you know that the models you are using today are at the heart of a historic legal battle?  
Current common sense regarding copyright might be literally "turned upside down"—a shockwave that is currently rocking the tech industry.

The tech giant Meta has taken the bold stance in court that "even the act of uploading pirated e-books via BitTorrent constitutes 'fair use' as long as it is for AI training."

It’s natural to frown and think, "Isn't that a bit of a stretch?" However, the outcome of this trial holds the destiny of the LLMs (Large Language Models) we will use tomorrow. The Shiro-Negi Tech editorial team dives deep into the logic behind this "extreme medicine" of an argument.

## 💡 The Frontline of Chaos: What’s Happening?

To understand the essence of this battle, there are three key points you need to know:

*   **The Forbidden Fruit called "Books3"**: The "Books3" dataset, which Meta allegedly used to train Llama, contains tens of thousands of pirated books. While authors have sued, calling this a "clear copyright infringement," Meta shows no signs of backing down.
*   **Logic that Justifies Even "Uploading"**: Normally, sharing pirated content is an automatic violation. However, Meta argues that the process of training an AI model to create "new value (intelligence)" constitutes "Transformative Use," which is distinct from simply reading the original book. In other words, their logic is that if the purpose is noble, the act of uploading during that process can be justified.
*   **Defining BitTorrent as "Just Efficiency"**: For Meta, using P2P networks to acquire data is merely an "efficient means of data collection." It is an ultra-aggressive stance that seeks to overwrite existing legal frameworks with the speed of the AI era. 🔥

## 🔧 Why Engineers Should Watch This "Quagmire"

This isn't just a dispute for legal departments. It is a vital issue directly linked to the front lines of development.

1.  **Determining the "Purity" of Training Data**:  
    Until now, engineers in Japan have been protected by the shield of Article 30-4 of the Copyright Act. However, if Meta's argument prevails in the U.S., the global "legal line" for data usage will be significantly lowered. This would mean the degree of freedom in the data we can access will expand explosively.
2.  **The "Right to Exist" for OSS Models**:  
    If open-source models like Llama are certified as "legally clean," a golden age of OSS models that both companies and individuals can use with peace of mind will arrive. Conversely, if this foundation crumbles, the development of OSS AI may enter a "winter era."

## 📊 Miracle Cure or Destroyer? (Pros & Cons)

Is Meta's claim an "accelerator" that speeds up AI evolution, or "explosives" that destroy the creative world?

*   **Pros**: AI development speed will be elevated to "god-like" levels. Vast amounts of text data, previously stalled by legal concerns, will be unlocked as the source of human intelligence.
*   **Cons**: There is a risk that the rights of authors and publishers, and "respect for creation," will be left behind. Intense backlash from creators could lead to a general distrust of the entire AI community. 😢
